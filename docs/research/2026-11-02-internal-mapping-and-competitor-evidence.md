# Internal mapping option and competitor evidence — 2026-11-02

Owner rejected large recurring subscriptions and asks whether YoRight can build its own multi-supplier mapping and what Trip.com, Almosafer and almatar use. This is feasibility research, not implementation or production approval.

## Proposed low-subscription design

Multiple suppliers can remain in scope without an external mapping subscription, by building an owner-controlled identity/cross-reference layer for a bounded initial catalogue. Store an internal property ID, supplier name and native property ID, evidence fields, match status, provenance and update history. Normalize authorized names/addresses/coordinates and consider phone/brand and building identity; neither name nor proximity alone establishes equivalence. Automatically suggest candidates, auto-accept only under validated conservative rules, and review ambiguous cases instead of merging distinct properties. Keep verified mappings and re-check feed changes, rebrands, closures, merges and splits. Any supplier-provided shared IDs are reusable only within granted rights, not an assumed free licence to a proprietary mapping database.

This does not require solving all room mapping on day one. Show one hotel with multiple supplier offers; preserve unknown/ambiguous room types as separate choices with clear conditions. Compare lowest final eligible hotel offers as starting prices without claiming they are the same room. For true like-for-like selection, compare verified room attributes and each rate's occupancy, board, refundability/deadlines, payment and mandatory charges. Matching the word Standard or a name embedding alone is insufficient. Unknown critical attributes must not be treated as equal. AI may propose correspondences for review; it is not sole authority for automatic merging.

Preserve supplier-native rate/booking IDs; recheck price, availability and conditions before booking. Use supplier-authorized images/descriptions with provenance and room links. No unauthorized scraping or redistribution of another mapper's database is part of this design.

Proposed first scope: two or three commercially approved suppliers, selected cities and properties, automatic property candidate matching plus review, and conservative room grouping only where evidence is complete. Expand automation after sample checks of false merges, missed matches and operator workload. Internal mapping trades subscription expense for development, maintenance, compute and review cost; no free or cheaper-global-equivalent claim or numeric estimate established. Prior code/clone/security/privacy gates remain.

- Trip.com Group: its 2025 annual report, published in 2026, explicitly describes proprietary search/transaction technology including a hotel matching system and model algorithms. https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0428/2026042803041.pdf (PDF page 78, printed 76). This supports a mixed own-tech/external-content picture, not a complete stack census.
- Almosafer: a direct April 23, 2025 interview with its Chief Digital & Technology Officer Qais Amori describes the proprietary hotel connectivity platform Enigma connecting over 35 suppliers and significant hotel/room mapping investment. https://tsh.io/blog/saudi-arabias-travel-tech-vision-2030. This is a dated primary executive account; it does not reveal all external mapping/content contracts or their current prices. GIATA's public supplier-code statistics contain Almosafer almosafer_odis; this establishes mapped supplier codes, not customer subscription/payment or use of GIATA for the whole retail app: https://content-statistics.giatamedia.com/statistics/latest.
- almatar: Gimmonix's own LinkedIn product listing for Mapping.Works names almatar among Featured customers. https://www.linkedin.com/products/gimmonix-mappingworks-by/. This is vendor-published usage evidence, not a disclosed contract, price or independent customer confirmation of the current full stack. An employee profile alone is not relied on as proof of a vendor contract.
